## Discovery & Deep Research — the two stages of a run

A NicheIQ run has two stages, and you choose how far to go. **Discovery** scans a
market and hands you a ranked, quote-backed shortlist of problems and the product
ideas worth building. **Deep Research** takes one idea from that shortlist and
pressure-tests it until you have a clear answer on whether to build it. You can stop
after Discovery, or take a single idea further. Either way, you never pay to research
an idea you've already ruled out.

### Discovery — finding what's worth your time

Discovery does four things, in order:

- **Listens to real discussions.** We read what people in your niche actually say in
  public: the threads, the complaints, the questions they post when something isn't
  working. Not survey answers, and not our own guesses about what the market wants.
- **Finds the real problems.** We pull out the problems people keep hitting and rank
  them by how much they actually hurt. Every problem comes with real quotes, so you
  can see who said it and where (more on how we verify those quotes below).
- **Maps the audience.** For each problem we work out who has it: what they already
  use and complain about, where they gather, and how they describe the pain in their
  own words. That's the line between "people seem annoyed" and "here is exactly who
  would pay to stop being annoyed."
- **Shortlists the ideas.** We turn the strongest problems into a short list of
  product ideas, each tied back to a problem you can see the evidence for. We
  deliberately push past the first-thoughts anyone would name in five seconds, so the
  obvious and already-built ideas don't crowd out the ones actually worth your time.
  Ideas are grounded in data that's actually obtainable (official registries, public
  sources — not wishful "we'll scrape it somehow"), and the shortlist mixes focused
  single-problem tools with **bundled products** that combine several validated problems
  into one workflow — the shape people actually pay for. An independent critic re-scores
  every idea, and even discards a first-pick in favor of a runner-up it judges stronger.

You walk away with a shortlist you can share, then pick the one idea you want to take
seriously.

### Deep Research — pressure-testing one idea

Deep Research takes the single idea you chose and goes deep. It answers the questions
that decide whether an idea is worth building:

- How big is the market, and is it big enough to bother?
- Who would you be competing with, and where's the gap they've left open?
- Are people actually searching for this, and how much of that traffic could you
  realistically win?
- Is interest growing or fading, and is now the right time to start?
- How would you make money, and how would you actually build it?

It ends with a plain **go / no-go call** and the real risks named out loud. Not a hype
piece, and not a wall of maybes. The goal is to tell you what we'd do if it were our
money and our weekend.

## Evidence & Quotes — how we back every pain point

Every problem in a NicheIQ report is backed by **real quotes from real public
discussions** — not paraphrases and not AI-written summaries. When we say people
are frustrated with something, we show you their own words and where they said it.

The hard part isn't finding quotes that mention a topic — it's making sure each
quote actually describes *the problem we're claiming*. A comment that praises a
product, or that's about a different issue entirely, shouldn't count as evidence
just because it uses the same words. So before any quote makes it into your
report, we verify it genuinely expresses that specific pain.

**What we guarantee:**

- **Real, attributed quotes.** Every pain point shows actual quotes from public
  discussions, tied back to their source.
- **On-point, not just on-topic.** A quote only counts as evidence if it truly
  describes the problem. Off-topic chatter and positive comments don't sneak in
  as "complaints."
- **Multiple independent voices.** Evidence has to come from several different
  people and threads, so one loud or repeated voice can't masquerade as a trend.
- **Honest about thin evidence.** When a problem only has a little real support,
  we tell you — and we hold its score back rather than inflate it. Our scoring
  only ever *lowers* confidence on weak evidence; it never pads.

In short: **if a quote doesn't clearly describe the problem, it doesn't make the
report.** You're reading the genuine voice of the market, not noise dressed up to
look like consensus.
